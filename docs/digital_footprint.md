# Digital Footprint

## What is a digital footprint?

A digital footprint is the trail of information that a person leaves behind when they use the internet & digital devices.

## The Two Types of Footprints

### 1. Active Digital Footprints

Data that a user **intentionally and deliberately** shares or publishes.

* **Public Communications:** Social media posts, forum comments, blog articles & public code repositories (e.g., GitHub).
* **User-Generated Content:** Photos, videos, and audio files uploaded to the public.
* **Forms & Registrations:** Signing up for newsletters, creating accounts or filling out online petitions.

### 2. Passive Digital Footprints

Data that is collected about a user **without their explicit execution or immediate awareness**.

* **Exif & Metadata:** Hidden data embedded in files, such as the exact GPS coordinates, timestamp & device model attached to an uploaded JPEG.
* **Network & Browser Identifiers:** IP addresses, DNS leak fragments, cookies & browser fingerprints.
* **Behavioral Analytics:** Server logs detailing exactly when you visited a site, how long you stayed & what links you hovered over.
* **Third-Party Aggregation:** Data scraped, packaged & sold by data brokers (e.g., voter registration links, property records, marketing profiles).

## Why is it important?

### **Reputation:** 

The internet acts as a permanent ledger. Future employers, educational institutions & automated background-checking algorithms parse public digital footprints to assess a candidate's character, alignments & professional history.

### **Privacy and security:** 

A large or poorly managed digital footprint expands an individual's attack surface. 
* **Targeted Phishing:** Malicious actors use footprint data (like hobbies, workplace details or pet names) to craft highly convincing social engineering attacks.
* **Credential Stuffing:** Reused usernames or leaked emails from historic data breaches allow attackers to pivot into critical, active accounts.
* **Physical Safety:** Geotagged images or check-ins expose real-world routines, frequently visited locations & living spaces.

### **Permanence:** 

Deleting a post or deleting an account does not erase a digital footprint. Because of web scrapers, data brokers, archival projects (like the Wayback Machine) & peer-to-peer sharing, digital information is practically permanent once it touches the public internet.
